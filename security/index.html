Security

What is true today.

A security page you cannot check is marketing. This one separates what is built from what is planned, and names the second list rather than hiding it.

What is in place

Everything in this list is built and running now, and every line of it is something we can show you rather than assert. If you are doing vendor due diligence, ask for the demonstration — a claim on a web page is not evidence, and treating it as evidence is how a supplier gets away with it.

  • Tenant isolation enforced at the database, not in application code. A missing clause in a query cannot leak another business's data, because the database itself will not return it.
  • Encryption in transit everywhere, and at rest for stored data and backups.
  • Role-based access inside a workspace, so your own team sees what their role needs and not the whole company's numbers by default.
  • Support access is granted by you, expires by itself, shows a banner in your workspace while it is active, and writes to your audit log — not only to ours.
  • Audit logging of the actions that matter: logins, permission changes, exports, deletions.
  • Backups taken on a schedule and held to a defined retention.

What is not built yet

This is the list most vendors leave off. It is here because you will find out eventually, and it is much better for both of us if you find out now.

  • No SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certification. We are not going to imply one with a badge graphic. If your procurement process requires a certificate, we will fail it today and you should know that before we both spend a month on it.
  • No formal penetration test report yet. Planned, and we will publish the fact of it when it happens.
  • Backup restores are tested by hand rather than on a schedule, and we have not had to do one under real pressure yet.
  • No customer-managed encryption keys.
  • No published uptime SLA with service credits, for the reason set out in the terms: we do not have enough operating history for the number to mean anything.
  • Single sign-on is not available yet.
  • This website has no forms on it. Every contact route here is an email address, which is why there is no bot protection to describe — a form that posts somewhere is a thing to secure, and until one exists we would rather not have it.

How an incident is handled

If something goes wrong, the first priority is containment and the second is telling you. Not the other way around, and not after a week of internal review.

Customers hear from us within 48 hours of us confirming an incident — what happened, what data was involved, what we have done, and what you need to do. Where the DPDP Act requires it, a detailed report goes to the Data Protection Board within 72 hours. For personal data inside your workspace, the duty to notify the individuals is yours, because you are the fiduciary; our job is to get you the facts fast enough that your own clock has not already run down when you start.

We would rather tell you about a near miss than have you find out about a real one from somebody else.

Reporting a vulnerability

If you have found something, please tell us. Write to hello@ordence.com with enough detail to reproduce it and we will acknowledge it within one business day.

We will not threaten you, we will not involve lawyers over a good-faith report, and we will credit you if you want the credit. There is no bounty programme yet — when there is, it will be on this page.
