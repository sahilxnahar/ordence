How we build

Almost every problem with a business website is a performance problem wearing a design problem's clothes. These are the decisions that fix it, and they are made before any design work starts.

  • Static first. Pages are built once and served as files, so there is no server to be slow and nothing to go down under load.
  • Globally cached. Your page is served from a data centre near your customer, not from one machine in one city.
  • No framework tax. A page of text is HTML and CSS, and it weighs what text weighs. Where something genuinely needs an engine — the home page of this site runs a live WebGL field and pays about 800KB for it — that page carries the cost and no other page does.
  • Accessible by default. Keyboard navigation, real contrast, sensible headings — which is also, not coincidentally, what search engines reward.
  • Measured, not asserted. We test on a mid-range Android phone on a normal Indian connection, because that is what your customers are actually holding.

How a project runs

Four stages, and you see something real at the end of each.

  1. Audit

    We go through what you have now — speed, structure, what it is costing you in lost enquiries. You get that written up whether or not you hire us.

  2. Scope

    A written document listing every page, every integration and every thing we are not doing, with a fixed price attached to it.

  3. Build

    You see it on a real URL from the first week, and it stays there and keeps changing. No six-week silence ending in a reveal.

  4. Handover

    Hosting in your own account, a recorded walkthrough so the next person to touch it is not starting from nothing, and the source — assigned to you outright where your scope sheet says so, licensed to you for as long as you are a customer where it does not. Which one it is is written down before we start.

Why this sits next to a CRM and an ERP

It looks like an odd thing to sell alongside business software, and there is a specific reason for it. The businesses we build systems for almost all had a website that a different agency built, that nobody could edit, and that had no connection to the software running the company. An enquiry arrived in an inbox and was copied into a spreadsheet by hand.

When the same team builds both, that gap does not exist. The form on the site writes straight into the pipeline, the product pages read from the same catalogue as the orders, and the brand does not quietly become two brands. If you only want the site, that is entirely fine — but this is why it is on the menu.
