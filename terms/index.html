What this is, and who it is between

Everything starts with a written scope and a fixed quote, and you become a customer when you sign that quote or its accompanying order form. Signing it means you accept these terms as well.

If you have a separately signed agreement with us, that agreement wins wherever it and this page differ. This page is not a replacement for it — it is the default set of terms, and the part of the contract that anyone at your company can read without asking a lawyer for a translation.

What a subscription includes

A subscription is a workspace on the Ordence platform, on your own subdomain or your own domain, with the modules named in your scope switched on. It is a licence to use the platform for as long as you are paying for it, not a purchase of software you keep.

  • The modules on your scope sheet — CRM, ERP, AI features, and anything else written there. Nothing else is promised, even if you have seen it on this website.
  • Hosting, backups, security updates and platform upgrades, at no extra charge. When we improve the platform, you get the improvement.
  • Named users up to the number in your quote. Adding people beyond that number changes the price, and we will tell you before it does, not after.
  • Support, on the terms in the support section below.
  • Your data, exportable in open formats, at any time, without asking us first.
  • Not included unless your scope says so: bespoke development, data migration from your old systems, on-site training, integrations with third-party services, and website or design work. Those are quoted separately.

The scope sheet, and the quote

Before you pay anything, we write down what you are getting. That document — the scope sheet — lists the modules, the number of users, what we are building for you if anything, what we are migrating, and what we are not doing. The quote attaches to it and gives a fixed price.

The scope sheet is the definition of the deal. If something is not on it, it is not in the price, and if you think it should be, the time to say so is before you sign rather than three months in. We would rather spend an extra week getting that document right than argue about it later.

The capability map on this website shows the shape of the platform, including parts of it that are still being built. It is not a list of what is finished and it is not a promise to you. Only your scope sheet is.

When the scope changes

Scope changes. You start using the thing and discover what you actually needed. That is normal and we do not treat it as a problem.

Small changes — a field, a report, a tweak to a workflow — we do as part of the subscription and do not bill for. We do not keep a stopwatch running on ordinary configuration.

Anything larger gets its own written change note: what is being added, what it costs, and whether it moves any date we have already agreed. Nothing gets built and nothing gets invoiced until you have said yes to that note in writing. Email is writing.

The same applies in reverse. If you want to drop a module or reduce users, tell us and we will re-quote from the next renewal. We will not reduce a running invoice mid-term, but we will not hold you to a module you have stopped using either.

Your data is yours

Everything your business puts into the workspace stays yours — customers, deals, orders, stock, invoices, documents, and every configuration you build on top of them. Nothing in this agreement transfers any of it to us, and nothing about it changes because an invoice is late or a contract is ending.

We hold that data to run the service for you and for nothing else. We do not sell it, we do not mine it, we do not use it to train models, and we do not use one customer's data to improve another customer's experience. If we ever want to do anything with it beyond running your workspace, we will ask first and take no for an answer.

There is a split in the law that matters here and that we keep deliberately separate. For the data inside your workspace, you are the Data Fiduciary under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and we are your Data Processor — it is your data, and we act on your instructions. For the handful of details this website collects from you, we are the fiduciary. The privacy policy sets out both roles in full, and the data processing terms sit alongside this agreement. Read those rather than assuming this page covers it, because it does not.

Questions or requests about personal data go to hello@ordence.com. Where the request is one we are answerable for, we reply within 30 days.

What you cannot do with it

This list is short on purpose. It is not a net cast wide enough to catch anything we later dislike — it is the specific things that would harm other customers, break the law, or damage the platform.

  • Do not put anything unlawful into the workspace, or use it to do anything unlawful.
  • Do not send bulk unsolicited messages from it, by email, SMS or WhatsApp. India has rules about this and so do the providers we sit behind. It is the quickest way to get a sending domain blocked for everyone.
  • Do not resell or sublicense the workspace, or give access to a business that is not yours, unless your scope sheet says you can.
  • Do not try to break, probe or overload the platform, or to get at another customer's data. If you have found a security problem, tell us — we would much rather have the report than the incident.
  • Do not copy the platform, decompile it, or use it to build a competing product.
  • Keep your team's access current. When someone leaves your company, remove them. We give you the tools to do it, but we cannot know it happened.
  • You are responsible for what your own people do inside your workspace, including the accuracy of what they type into it.

Uptime and support, stated honestly

We aim for the platform to be up whenever your team is working, and we run it as if it were our own business on it. Planned maintenance is announced in advance and scheduled outside Indian working hours wherever we can.

We do not publish an uptime percentage and we do not offer a service credit SLA, because we have not been running long enough for either number to mean anything. A guaranteed 99.9 percent from a small team with no track record is a marketing claim, not a commitment, and you would be right not to believe it. When we have real numbers, we will publish them here, and at that point we will offer a service level you can hold us to.

Support is by email and phone during [[SUPPORT HOURS]]. It is a small team, so what you get is a person who knows the product and your account rather than a ticket queue and a script. We aim to acknowledge anything you raise within [[ACKNOWLEDGE HOURS]] working hours, and to get to something that has stopped your business working the same day.

If we are going to miss something, we would rather tell you than let a deadline pass quietly. That is the whole support philosophy, and it is the part we would ask you to judge us on.

Who owns what

Three different things are easy to lump together here, and they have three different answers.

  • The platform is ours. The Ordence software, its design, its code, its name and its brand stay ours. A subscription is permission to use it, not a transfer of it, and that permission ends when the agreement does.
  • Your data is yours, without qualification, as set out above. So is any configuration your team builds on top of it — custom fields, workflows, templates, reports. It exports with the rest.
  • Bespoke work depends on what we agreed. Where we build something specifically for you and your scope sheet says it is yours, it is yours on full payment for it. Otherwise the default is that we keep ownership of what we build and you get a licence to use it for as long as you are a customer. Whichever it is will be written on the scope sheet before we start, not decided afterwards.
  • We keep the right to reuse the general knowledge, techniques and reusable components that come out of any project. What we will never reuse is anything specific to you: your data, your process, your configuration, your name.
  • If you send us feedback or an idea for the product, we may build it, and we do not owe you anything for it. Most of what is good in Ordence came from a customer saying something was annoying.
  • Anything you upload — your logo, your content, your documents — stays yours. You are giving us only the permission we need to host it and show it back to you.

Confidentiality, both ways

Running your business on our software means we see things — your customers, your margins, your suppliers, how you actually work. We treat all of it as confidential. We do not discuss it outside the people who need it to do the job, we do not use it for anything except serving you, and we do not name you as a customer publicly without asking first.

The same obligation runs the other way. Our pricing, our roadmap, anything we show you under the terms of a demo or a proposal, and anything we mark confidential is yours to keep to yourself.

The usual exceptions apply on both sides: information that is already public, that you already had, that someone else gave you without a duty of confidence, or that a court or regulator requires to be disclosed. If we are ever ordered to hand over something of yours, we will tell you before we do it unless the law forbids us from telling you.

This survives the end of the agreement. It does not stop when the invoices do.

What we promise, and what we do not

We promise that we will provide the service with reasonable skill and care, that we have the right to license you what we are licensing you, and that we will do what your scope sheet says we will do. If we get something wrong, we will fix it, and fixing it is the first response rather than a negotiation.

We do not promise the software is free of bugs, because no software is. We do not promise it will never be down. We do not promise it will fit a purpose you have not told us about, or that it will produce a particular commercial outcome for your business — we cannot control how you run it.

AI features come with a specific caveat that we would rather state plainly than bury. They draft, summarise and suggest. They are sometimes wrong, and they can be confidently wrong. Anything an AI feature produces is a draft for a person to check, not an authority to act on. Do not send a customer a number that no human has read.

Beyond what is written in this section, and to the extent the law lets us say so, we do not give any other warranty, express or implied.

The limit on what we owe you

If we cause you a loss, our total liability across everything to do with this agreement is capped at the fees you have actually paid us in the twelve months before the claim. That is a real limit and we are stating it up front rather than in a paragraph you would have to hunt for.

Neither side is liable to the other for indirect losses, lost profits, lost revenue, lost business opportunity or reputational damage. Those are real costs, but they are not costs either of us can price when signing.

We are not liable for what your own team enters incorrectly, for how you choose to run your business on the software, or for third-party services you connect to it and we did not supply.

Some things cannot be capped and we are not trying to cap them: fraud, wilful misconduct, death or personal injury caused by negligence, a breach of confidentiality, and anything else the law does not permit to be limited. The cap also does not apply to fees you owe us.

This is where a general counsel usually earns their fee, so if you have one, this is the section to hand them.

How long it runs, and how either side ends it

You can end it at renewal, for any reason or none, by giving that notice. You do not have to explain yourself and we will not put you through a retention conversation.

We may also end it for repeated non-payment, or for use that puts other customers at risk, after the notice described in the fees and acceptable use sections.

If we ever decide to stop offering the product, you will get at least [[WIND-DOWN NOTICE DAYS]] days notice, a refund of the unused part of anything you have prepaid, and help getting your data somewhere else.

What happens when it ends

The end of a contract is where software companies behave worst, so this part is deliberately specific. Nothing here is designed to make leaving difficult.

  • Full access to the workspace ends on the last day of the term, or on the termination date if it ended early. It does not go dark: for the thirty days that follow you can still sign in to export, which is the only thing that account can then do.
  • Export first. Export is self-serve, in open formats, and available at any time — including during the notice period and including while an account is suspended for non-payment. We do not hold records hostage over an invoice.
  • If you would rather we did the export for you, ask, and we will hand over a full dump of your data in a usable format. For a standard export we do not charge for this.
  • You have thirty days from the end date to get everything out. During those thirty days your data is still there and we can still help you reach it.
  • After thirty days we delete our copies, including from backups on their normal rotation cycle. Deletion is on our side only; a copy you exported is yours to manage, and we cannot delete that for you.
  • If you need longer than thirty days, ask before the window closes. We would rather extend it than have you lose something. Once it is deleted we cannot get it back, and we will not pretend otherwise.
  • We keep what tax and company law requires us to keep — invoices, contracts, statutory records — for as long as those laws require, and nothing beyond that.
  • Anything either side already owes the other still has to be paid. Confidentiality, the ownership terms and the liability cap all survive.

Changes to these terms

We may update these terms as the product and the law change. If a change actually affects you, we will tell customers directly and give at least [[CHANGE NOTICE DAYS]] days notice before it takes effect. We will not quietly edit the page and move the date at the top.

If a change materially reduces what you are getting and you do not want to accept it, tell us within that notice period and you can end the agreement at that point with a refund of the unused part of anything you have prepaid.

Changes never apply backwards, and they never change the price inside a term you have already paid for.

Governing law, and where a dispute goes
