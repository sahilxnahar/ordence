What it does

Operations, in the order the work happens.

  • Orders

    An accepted quote becomes an order with its lines intact. Nobody re-keys anything, so nothing is transcribed wrong at the point it starts to matter.

  • Stock

    Live quantities across locations, with reservations against open orders — so what you can promise is what you actually have, not what the last count said.

  • Purchase

    Purchase orders, goods receipt and supplier bills, with a three-way match. A discrepancy surfaces the day it happens rather than at the end of the quarter.

  • Invoicing and GST

    GST-compliant invoices with HSN codes, the CGST/SGST versus IGST split handled by place of supply, and e-invoice and e-way-bill support where your turnover requires it.

  • Payments and receivables

    What is outstanding, how old it is, and who has been chased. Ageing that updates itself instead of being rebuilt in Excel every Friday.

  • Reports on one set of numbers

    Margin, ageing, stock value and order book all read the same ledger. There is no reconciliation step because there is nothing to reconcile.

Why one ledger and not five systems

Every business we have worked with had the same architecture without meaning to: sales in one place, stock in another, invoices in a third, and a person whose actual job is copying between them. That person is not the problem. The gaps are.

Each copy is a chance to type a number wrong, and each system holds a version of the truth that was correct at the moment it was written and has been drifting since. The end of the month is not slow because accounting is slow. It is slow because somebody has to decide which of three systems was right.

One ledger removes the question. A quantity is a quantity. When the stock moves, the valuation moves, and the report showing you margin does not need to be told.

Questions people actually ask

We already use Tally. Does this replace it?

Usually not at first, and we will say so rather than sell you a rip-and-replace. Most businesses run Ordence for operations and keep Tally for statutory books, with a clean export between them. Once that is stable, some move the books across too.

How long does it take to go live?

For a standard scope, weeks rather than quarters — the honest range depends almost entirely on how clean your existing stock and customer data is. We tell you which end of the range you are at after looking at your data, not before.

What happens to our historical data?

Open orders, current stock and customer masters come across. Full transaction history usually stays where it is and gets archived, because migrating five years of it costs more than it is ever worth.

Is it compliant?

It produces GST-compliant documents and supports e-invoicing and e-way bills. Compliance is a moving target and we would rather show you the current state against your specific turnover and state than make a blanket claim on a web page.
