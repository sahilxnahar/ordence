What it does

Six things, and they are the six that go wrong first.

  • One inbox for every channel

    Website form, WhatsApp, phone, email, IndiaMART, a card someone was handed at an exhibition. All of it lands in one queue instead of six half-watched ones.

  • An owner within seconds

    Rules assign by territory, product, deal size or round-robin. An enquiry that nobody owns is the single most expensive object in a sales team.

  • A pipeline that tells the truth

    Stages you define, with an age on every card. A deal that has not moved in three weeks looks different from one that moved yesterday.

  • Quotes from the same data

    Build a quote against the customer record, send it as a PDF, and have the accepted version become the order. No re-typing into a separate template.

  • Follow-ups that do not depend on memory

    Every deal carries its next action and its date. The morning list is the deals that are due, not a spreadsheet somebody has to maintain.

  • One timeline per customer

    Calls, quotes, orders, invoices and payments against a single record — so whoever picks up the phone knows the whole history before they say hello.

How it actually runs

A working week, in the order it happens.

  1. Monday morning

    Everyone opens to their own list: deals due today, deals gone quiet, new enquiries assigned overnight. Not a dashboard of numbers — a list of things to do.

  2. An enquiry arrives

    It is captured, de-duplicated against existing customers, assigned by your rules, and acknowledged. If nobody has touched it within the window you set, it escalates.

  3. The deal moves

    Stage changes are one action. Notes, calls and files attach to the record rather than to somebody's laptop.

  4. It closes

    Won deals hand off to ERP as an order with the quoted lines already on it. Lost deals capture a reason, which is the only way you ever learn anything from them.

Built for how Indian sales teams actually work

Most CRMs are built for a company selling software in a currency that is not rupees. The differences are small and they are the reason adoption fails.

  • WhatsApp is a first-class channel, not an integration someone bolted on. Most of your customers will never send you an email.
  • Phone numbers are the identity that matters. Records de-duplicate on the number, not on an email address half your contacts do not use.
  • Quotes carry GST correctly, including the CGST/SGST versus IGST split, so what sales sends out matches what accounts issues.
  • It works on a phone properly, because a large part of your team is not at a desk.
  • Regional-language notes and customer names are stored and searched properly, not mangled into question marks.

Questions people actually ask

Will my team use it?

That is the only question that matters, and the honest answer is that they will use it if it is faster than what they do now and not if it is slower. So we scope the first version to be small: capture, assign, follow up. Reporting comes after people are actually in it daily.

Can we import from our spreadsheet?

Yes, and it is usually the first thing we do. Send us the sheet before you sign and we will tell you what will import cleanly and what will not.

What about our existing WhatsApp Business number?

It can be connected. There are limits set by Meta rather than by us, and we will walk through them honestly before you plan anything around it.

Do we have to take the ERP as well?

No. The CRM works on its own. It works better with the ERP because a won deal becomes an order without anyone re-typing it, but that is an argument, not a requirement.
