What it is for

Removing typing and watching, not making decisions.

  • Drafting

    The follow-up email, the quote covering note, the payment reminder. Written against the actual record, in your tone, for a person to read and send.

  • Asking in plain language

    "Which customers in Pune have not ordered since March?" You get the answer and the list of records it came from, so you can check it.

  • Alerts worth having

    A deal gone quiet, stock about to run out against open orders, an invoice ageing past its terms. Told to you before it is a problem rather than after.

  • Summarising

    A customer's whole history in three lines before you call them. Ten minutes of scrolling, removed.

  • Cleaning up data

    Finding the duplicates, the missing GSTINs, the phone numbers with a stray digit. Boring, high-value, and nobody has ever had time to do it.

The three rules it is built on

Most AI in business software is a chat box with a general model behind it and your data pasted into a prompt. That is easy to build and it is why nobody trusts it. These three constraints are what we hold to instead.

  • Grounded, not general. It answers from your records. If the answer is not in your data, it says it does not know rather than producing something plausible.
  • Cited, always. Every claim links to the deal, invoice or stock movement it came from. An answer you cannot check is not an answer, it is a rumour with formatting.
  • A person decides. It drafts, suggests and flags. Nothing is sent, approved, posted or paid without a human. The AI removes the typing, never the judgement.

What we will not do with your data

Your records are not training data. We do not train models on your workspace, we do not use one customer's data to improve another customer's experience, and we do not let your data leave the processing needed to answer your own question.

This matters more than it sounds. The reason most Indian businesses are quietly nervous about AI features is not the technology — it is not knowing where their customer list ends up. The privacy policy sets out the sub-processors involved by name, and the terms make the training commitment contractual rather than a claim on a marketing page.

Language models get things wrong, sometimes confidently. Everything the AI produces is a draft for a person to check. If a feature ever cannot be checked easily, that is a reason not to ship the feature.

Questions people actually ask

Does it work in Hindi or other Indian languages?

For reading and summarising, yes, and it handles mixed-script notes reasonably. For drafting customer-facing text we would want to show you output in your specific language before you rely on it.

Can I switch it off?

Entirely, per workspace or per module. Some businesses want the alerts and none of the drafting. That is a supported configuration, not a special case.

What does it cost?

It is part of the platform rather than a per-seat upsell. Where a specific high-volume use pushes real cost, we say so during scoping instead of discovering it on your invoice.

Is this actually built?

Parts of it. Drafting, summarising and alerts are the mature end; open-ended plain-language querying is the newest and the one we would show you working on your own data before you count on it. Your scope sheet says what is live for you.

Ask it something you already know the answer to.

That is the only fair test of a system like this — give it a question you can check, and see whether the citation holds up.
