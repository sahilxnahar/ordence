The same system, over and over

I kept building the same thing for client after client. Sales in WhatsApp, stock in a spreadsheet, invoices in Tally, and one person spending the first week of every month working out which of them was right.

Every time, the fix was the same shape: get the records into one place, make the handoffs automatic, and stop asking people to be the integration layer. And every time, it was rebuilt from scratch, because the products that already existed were either built for a ten-person startup in San Francisco or for a company with a procurement department and an implementation partner.

There is a very large gap between those two, and almost every business in India that employs between ten and a hundred people is sitting in it. Ordence is what I built for that gap instead of building it again by hand.

What we are trying to be

Not principles on a wall. These are the decisions we have actually made, and each one has cost us something.

  • Honest about what is built. The security page lists what we have not done. The AI page says which parts are new. This costs us deals and it is worth it.
  • Priced by scope, not by seat count alone, because a per-seat price would misprice most of the businesses we serve.
  • Your data, always exportable, including while an invoice is overdue. Lock-in by friction is not a business model we want.
  • Built for how Indian businesses actually work — WhatsApp, phone numbers as identity, GST that is correct, a phone-first team.
  • Small on purpose. There is no account-manager layer between you and the person who can fix your problem.

Who you will deal with

Me, directly, before you sign and after. That is a real advantage and a real limit, and you should weigh both.

The advantage: nothing is lost in translation between the person who understood your problem and the person who builds the solution, because they are the same person. You will not be handed to a support tier that has never seen your configuration.

The limit: we are small. We cannot take twenty implementations at once, and we would rather tell you we have no capacity until March than take your money and be late. If you need a vendor with a bench of forty consultants and a 24/7 follow-the-sun desk, that vendor exists and it is not us.

Built in India

Not as a slogan — as a set of technical decisions. GST is not an add-on module. WhatsApp is a first-class channel rather than an integration. Phone numbers are the identity a record de-duplicates on. Regional-language text is stored and searched properly instead of becoming question marks in a database that was never configured for it. The interface is tested on a mid-range Android phone on a normal connection.

Every one of those is a small thing. Together they are the difference between software that fits how you work and software you have to work around.

Ask me something difficult.

What is not built yet, what we are bad at, why a competitor might suit you better. I would rather answer that now than after you have signed.
