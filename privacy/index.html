Privacy

What we collect, and why.

This covers two different things that are easy to confuse, and that the law treats differently: the handful of details we collect from you as a visitor to this website, and the business data inside a customer's workspace. For the first we are the one answerable for it. For the second it is yours, not ours, and we only do what you tell us to. This page says exactly what each of those means in practice.

Two roles, and which one you are reading about

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025 split responsibility between a Data Fiduciary — whoever decides why personal data is collected — and a Data Processor, who handles it on the fiduciary's instructions. Ordence is both, in two clearly separate places, and conflating them would let us make promises we have no standing to keep.

The distinction is not academic. If you are an individual whose details sit in a customer's workspace, we cannot lawfully decide to delete them on your say-so; only the business that put them there can. If you are a visitor to this website, we decide, and you can ask us directly.

  • Data Fiduciary — for this website. Contact enquiries, workspace requests, and anything we send you afterwards. We decide what is collected and why, so the duty to answer you is ours.
  • Data Processor — for a customer workspace. The records a customer's business puts into the product, including personal data about their own customers and staff. The customer is the Data Fiduciary there; we act only on their documented instructions.

Role one: what this website collects

Today this website has no forms on it. Every route to us is an email address, so what we hold about you is the email you chose to send: your name, whatever address you sent it from, and whatever you wrote. It goes to one person's inbox. We use it to reply to you and to prepare a quote — nothing else. When a contact form does ship, it will collect the same things and this paragraph will say so before the form goes live, not after.

We also see the ordinary technical details any web server sees while serving a page — an IP address, a browser string, the page requested. We do not build a profile from them and we do not join them to anything else.

We do not run advertising trackers, we do not sell or share this with anyone, and we do not add you to a mailing list because you asked a question. Consent you give here can be withdrawn at any time, by the same means you gave it — one email, no form, no retention team. Withdrawing it does not undo processing that already happened, and if you would rather we did not keep the enquiry after we have replied, say so and we will delete it.

Role two: what a customer workspace holds

Your workspace holds the records your business puts into it — customers, deals, orders, stock, invoices, documents, and whatever else your modules cover. Some of that will be personal data about your own customers and staff. You decided to collect it, you decided why, and under the Act that makes you the Data Fiduciary for it.

We hold and process it on your instructions and for no other purpose. We do not mine it, we do not use it to train models, and we do not use one customer's data to improve another customer's experience. We do not sell it, and we will not use it to build anything of our own.

The instructions that bind us are the ones in your signed agreement and its data processing terms, plus whatever you do in the product itself. If we ever received an instruction we believed was unlawful, we would tell you rather than quietly carry it out.

If an individual writes to us about data in your workspace, we do not act on it. We tell them to contact you, and we tell you the same day that they asked, with enough detail for you to answer them inside your own deadline.

Where it lives, and whether it leaves India

Customer data is stored in [[DATA REGIONS]]. The table above says the same thing vendor by vendor, and if any of it sits outside India that is where you will see it.

The Act permits transfer outside India except to countries the Central Government restricts, and we will not move data to a restricted country. If we ever need to change where data is held, customers hear about it before it happens, not in a changelog afterwards.

Who can see it inside Ordence

Separation between workspaces is enforced by the database rather than by a rule in our application code that someone could forget to write. Two businesses on the platform never see a pixel of each other.

Our own staff cannot enter your workspace silently. Support access has to be granted by you, it expires, a banner shows in your workspace for as long as it is active, and it is written to your audit log — not only to ours. That is deliberate: it means you can check this claim rather than take it.

The security page sets out the safeguards themselves — encryption, access control, logging — and is candid about the ones we have not built yet.

How long we keep it

Workspace data is kept while your account is open. When it closes you have thirty days to export whatever you have not already taken, and after those thirty days we delete our copies — including from backups on their normal rotation. Deletion is on our side; if you have exported a copy, that copy is yours to manage. The terms set the same window out clause by clause.

Website enquiries are kept for one year from the last time we heard from you, or until you ask us to remove them, whichever is sooner. That figure comes from the DPDP Rules, which cap retention at one year of inactivity for the classes of fiduciary they name and require 48 hours' notice to the person before data is erased on that basis. We apply it whether or not we fall in a named class: a year after your last contact we write to you, and if we hear nothing we delete it. An enquiry that became a customer relationship is governed by that relationship instead, and by the retention terms in your agreement.

We keep what tax and company law requires us to keep — invoices, contracts, statutory records — for as long as those laws require, and nothing beyond that.

Your rights, and how to use them

If we are the Data Fiduciary — that is, for website data — write to hello@ordence.com and we will answer within 30 days. You do not need a lawyer, a format, or a reason.

If your data sits in a workspace we host, the business running that workspace is the right first stop, because they decide what is held and we cannot overrule them. Write to us anyway if you are stuck, and we will help them answer you.

Withdrawing consent has to be as easy as giving it, and it is. One line by email is enough, and we will not ask you to justify it or route you through a retention conversation.

  • Access — ask what we hold about you, what we did with it, and who else received it.
  • Correction, completion and updating — have something fixed if it is wrong.
  • Erasure — have it deleted, unless a law requires us to keep it.
  • Withdrawal of consent — at any time, as easily as you gave it.
  • Grievance redressal — a named person who has to answer you, before you need to go anywhere else.
  • Nomination — name someone who can exercise these rights for you if you die or become incapable of it.

If there is a breach

If personal data we hold is exposed, we tell the people affected without delay and in plain words: what happened, what data, what we have done, and what you should do. Customers hear from us within 48 hours of us confirming an incident, and a detailed report goes to the Data Protection Board within 72 hours as the Act requires.

For workspace data the notification duty to the individuals is yours, because you are the fiduciary. Our job is to get you the facts fast enough that your own clock is not already running out when you start. The security page describes how we handle an incident.

Children's data

Ordence is business software sold to businesses. This website is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone under eighteen through it.

If your business processes children's data in a workspace, the Act's additional duties fall on you as the fiduciary: verifiable parental consent, no tracking or behavioural monitoring of children, and no targeted advertising to them. We will not stop you entering that data, and we cannot verify consent on your behalf — so if that is your situation, tell us before you sign and we will go through what the product does and does not do about it.

Cookies

This website sets one cookie, which remembers that you have dismissed the notice at the bottom of the screen. That is the whole list. There are no advertising cookies, no third-party analytics scripts, and nothing that follows you to another site.

When forms do ship here they will be protected by Cloudflare Turnstile, which checks that a submission comes from a person rather than a script. It will see your IP address and some browser signals for that one check, on those pages only. It is not analytics, which is why it will sit outside the consent notice rather than behind it: it protects the very thing you are choosing to send us. It is listed in the table above ahead of time rather than added quietly on the day.

Where the law is, and where we are

The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 with a staggered phase-in of about eighteen months. Consent-manager registration comes in at twelve months; the core obligations — consent notices, retention and erasure, security safeguards — bite around May 2027. Most of this page is therefore ahead of the deadline rather than behind it, which is the point.

We are not claiming to be finished. Where a commitment here is a commitment rather than a description of something already built, we have tried to say so, and the security page keeps a running list of what we have not done yet.
